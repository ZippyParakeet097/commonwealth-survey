The Glowing Sea

Farthest reach
Inside game map
Theorized whole

The game's Glowing Sea was traced from the terrain colour of the satellite map, where the ash plain shows as a distinct blue-grey patch, and warped onto the real ground like everything else. It fills the southwest corner of the playable area and runs straight into the map's west and south edges, so the game only shows part of it. Everything beyond the map edge below is a theory, drawn with sparser hatching.

Measured from the Crater of Atom, the visible edge sits away: nearest to the east and northeast, toward Boston, and farthest to the north. The crater is not in the middle. The edge curves round a point to its southwest, the lopsided egg a fallout footprint makes, with ground zero near the narrow upwind end. Fitted to the visible edge, that shape puts the fallout drifting toward the southwest (bearing ), so the hidden side beyond the map is the long, downwind end, not a thin rim.

Wind. That needs a northeast wind on the morning of the war, and it is not the usual one. On late-October mornings from 2000 to 2020, the ground wind at Norwood blew from the northeast or east about one morning in six (17%), the lowest 3 km of air over Chatham moved toward the southwest quadrant about one morning in nine (11%), and the high winds that carry a megaton cloud's fine fallout went that way almost never (1%). Most days the fallout would have gone toward Boston. A clear, crisp day with high pressure building in from Quebec gives exactly that northeast breeze, which suits the sunny morning in the game's opening.

Ground. The terrain decides where it stops. The crater sits in a low basin of swamps and river meadows, about above sea level, drained by the Charles, the Neponset and the Stop River. Southwest of the map edge that lowland runs on through Medway, Franklin, Wrentham and Plainville, glacial sand and gravel threaded with swamp, until it meets the rocky, till-covered hills of Holliston, Franklin and Wrentham and, past them, the Worcester Plateau rising toward Hopkinton and Milford at around . The theorized edge treats high ground as a barrier and wet low ground as a channel, so it follows the valleys out and halts at the hills: its hidden stretch runs mostly at , reaching south into . If the hills had held nothing back, the same footprint would run on (dotted line, from the crater).

How well each version matches the part of the Sea the game shows (area overlap, intersection over union): a crater-centred circle , the wind-blown footprint over real terrain , the same footprint on flat ground . The game's zone does not follow real hills, so terrain costs a little fit inside the map; it is used for where the hidden side would stop. In the model, ground gets e-fold harder to cross for every 60 m (200 ft) it rises above the crater floor, and swamp and open water are 40% easier. The whole Sea comes to : no wind, then with terrain (drawn), then flat ground.

Its bomb was not the only one. Cambridge crater, a block-wide bowl on the East Cambridge riverfront, is drawn the same way. Sized against Fenway on the game map, it is about across against for the Crater of Atom. In dry soil, a surface burst's crater widens roughly with yield to the 0.3 power, so those sizes point to about at Cambridge and in the Sea: one bomb about times bigger than the other. Each crater is drawn at that size, with a dashed ring for its lip of thrown-out earth.

Two of Boston's rivers drain it. The Charles winds through Millis and Medfield inside the zone before heading for the city, and the Neponset rises inside it and reaches the harbor between Dorchester and Quincy.

Winds: Chatham (KCHH) 8 am soundings and Norwood airport observations, 9 October to 6 November, 2000–2020, from the Iowa Environmental Mesonet. Terrain: AWS Terrain Tiles (USGS elevation). Wetlands: MassGIS 1:25,000 hydrography.

Crater yields use Glasstone and Dolan, The Effects of Nuclear Weapons, §6.09: a dry-soil surface burst leaves a crater about 60 ft in radius at 1 kiloton, growing as the yield to the 0.3 power. The game's crater art is the only evidence, so treat them as rough.

The southwest corner has no real landmarks to pin the warp, so this part of the sheet is the least certain: read the edge as good to a mile or two.

      About the survey

      The game squeezes eastern Massachusetts into a few square miles. This survey puts every game location back on the real ground, so you can measure journeys, frontiers and sight lines in true miles. Real landmarks sit where they really are, drawn to their real outlines when you zoom in. Fictional places are placed by stretching the game map to fit them.

      Reading the scale

      Turn on the scale grid to see the ground in even squares, and pick a square size to suit the question: a quarter mile for a settlement's walls, a mile for a day's patrol, five or ten for a faction's reach. The grid covers the game map and the theorized Glowing Sea beyond its edge, with square A1 at the northwest corner. The place card names the square each location falls in, so you can cite a grid reference in your own writing.

      How the positions were made

      55 game locations have a real counterpart, such as Fenway Park for Diamond City, Fort Independence for the Castle, Long Island Head for Fort Strong and Scollay Square for Goodneighbor. Some are exact; others, like Harvard Square for College Square, are likely analogues, and the place card says which. A thin-plate spline fitted through those pairs, plus 11 fictional sites placed by hand from the game's clues (Hangman's Alley, the Nakano residence and others), moves everything else. Tested by leaving each anchor out in turn, it lands a typical place within about 0.7 miles of the truth, and never more than about 2.8.

      Places that sit on or under another one, like the Institute under CIT or Vault 114 under Park Street, take that site's position. Fictional land sites that the warp dropped into the sea were moved to the nearest shore, and their cards say so.

      Use the data

      Every position is in locations.csv with real latitude and longitude, and the Glowing Sea and craters are in glowing-sea.geojson, ready for QGIS, Google Earth or your own maps. Spotted a place in the wrong spot? Corrections are welcome on GitHub.

      Sources: MassGIS town boundaries and hydrography (1:25,000); OpenStreetMap for landmark positions, outlines and site plans, © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL; AWS Terrain Tiles (USGS elevation); the game's location table on the 2048 grid.
